Brand kit or brand designer? An honest comparison

A template and a commissioned brand identity solve different problems. The right choice depends on the maturity of the business, its requirements and the importance of a distinct, exclusive identity.

What a designer actually gives you

A good identity designer can develop a strategic, distinctive visual system around a business, its competitors and its audience. The scope may include original marks, usage rules, file formats and guidelines that general-purpose templates do not provide.

Why it's often wasted on a brand-new brand

Early-stage businesses often refine their positioning as they learn. A flexible starting system can be useful while a business is testing its offer, provided that the risk of looking similar to others is understood.

What a premium kit gives you instead

A well-designed kit can organize palettes, typography and layouts without requiring a fully bespoke identity. It may be easier to adapt, but it does not provide the same level of originality, exclusivity or research as commissioned design. The tradeoff is different for every project.

Our honest recommendation

For simple, short-term materials, an adaptable template may be sufficient. For a distinctive identity, complex brand system or high-stakes communication, commissioning a designer may be more appropriate. Start by defining what the visuals need to accomplish.
